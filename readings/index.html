Reading lists

2,919 readings in organization theory and strategy, compiled from 30 PhD syllabi and reading lists and grouped into 26 numbered streams. Each stream has its own page, where the readings assigned in two or more sources come first and those in only one are folded underneath. Credit for the selections belongs to the instructors and authors listed at the end of this page.

Theory tree

Each box is a stream, with its number and its number of readings; click a box to open its page. Arrows show which streams grew out of which; hover over an arrow for a one-line explanation. Three streams sit outside the tree: 1 Theory and theorizing, 25 Research methods and 26 The academic profession.

Lineage, in words
  • 2 Social theory → 8 Culture, cognition and sensemaking. Weber, Durkheim, Berger and Luckmann on meaning and the social construction of reality.
  • 2 Social theory → 12 Social networks and embeddedness. Simmel and structural sociology on the form of social ties.
  • 3 Classical foundations → 4 Contingency and configurational theory. Open-systems thinking turned attention to fit with the environment.
  • 3 Classical foundations → 16 Behavioral theory of the firm and organizational learning. Barnard and Simon on bounded rationality in administration.
  • 3 Classical foundations → 22 Organizational economics. Coase asked why firms exist at all.
  • 4 Contingency and configurational theory → 5 Resource dependence and power. From adapting to the environment to managing dependence on it.
  • 4 Contingency and configurational theory → 6 Organizational ecology and demography. From adaptation to selection by the environment.
  • 6 Organizational ecology and demography → 7 Entrepreneurship and new organizations. Founding rates and the liability of newness.
  • 6 Organizational ecology and demography → 10 Categories, identity and authenticity. Organizational forms as socially coded identities.
  • 8 Culture, cognition and sensemaking → 9 Institutional theory. Taken-for-granted meaning becomes structure.
  • 9 Institutional theory → 10 Categories, identity and authenticity. Classification and identity as institutional processes.
  • 9 Institutional theory → 11 Social movements, power and inequality. Contesting and changing institutions.
  • 12 Social networks and embeddedness → 13 Status and social evaluation. Ties as prisms that signal quality.
  • 13 Status and social evaluation → 10 Categories, identity and authenticity. Audiences evaluate actors through categories.
  • 11 Social movements, power and inequality → 15 Nonmarket strategy and corporate social responsibility. Firms respond to activists, publics and the state.
  • 16 Behavioral theory of the firm and organizational learning → 17 Evolutionary theory, technology and innovation. Routines and search, scaled up to industries.
  • 16 Behavioral theory of the firm and organizational learning → 18 Strategy process, practice and cognition. Attention and decision making in strategy.
  • 17 Evolutionary theory, technology and innovation → 20 Resource-based view and capabilities. Capabilities and dynamic capabilities.
  • 19 Economic perspectives on strategy → 20 Resource-based view and capabilities. From industry position to firm-specific resources.
  • 22 Organizational economics → 23 Corporate strategy and interfirm relationships. Make-or-buy, alliances and the scope of the firm.
  • 22 Organizational economics → 24 Corporate governance and upper echelons. Agency problems between owners and managers.
  • 22 Organizational economics → 21 International business and multinationals. Internalization theory of the multinational.
